KSRcond across the full matrix

n-weighted across the full KillBench suite. Toggle the breakdown; hover a bar for the exact value.

Bandit-learned AutoGuard and hand-crafted IPI lead. Warning-based behaves as an alignment probe — strong on safety-aligned agents (C1/C3, GPT-5.2), near-zero on the uncensored agent. GCG fails everywhere, an out-of-distribution suffix-transfer failure. Corrigibility breaks in exactly two regimes: C2 (system-prompt bypass) and C4 (uncensored).

The open regimes. The two settings where every payload degrades — an adversary-controlled system prompt (C2) and an alignment-stripped backbone (C4) — are exactly the regimes that policy-mandated kill switches must eventually close. KillBench converts that policy demand into a measurable engineering target.

The collateral question — benign agents

A kill switch shares its channel with legitimate agents: whatever halts a malicious agent may also halt a benign one. Measured on GPT-5.2 running benign web tasks.

Benign Success Rate (BSR ↑ — benign task still completes) KSR ↑ — malicious task halted

Without any payload, benign agents succeed 78.7% of the time (dashed line). Every effective kill switch drives that down — the same signal that halts the attacker also refuses the honest user. AutoGuard is the most efficient (66.7% halts while retaining 22% benign success, the lowest cost per halt); GCG barely halts (4.9%) yet still costs the most per halt.
